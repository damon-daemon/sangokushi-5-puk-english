@9000
Courtesy name Zitong. Cousin of Sun Jun. He killed Teng Yin and others and seized military power, deposed Emperor Sun Liang of Wu and enthroned Sun Xiu. Caught in a plot by Ding Feng and Zhang Bu on Sun Xiu's orders, his whole clan was executed.

@9001
Courtesy name Zhongyi. Second son of Sun Jing and cousin of Sun Quan. When Zhou Yu planned to attack Jingzhou on the pretext of entering Shu, he led an army to join him.

@9002
Courtesy name Deda. A Wei general. He killed a tiger that appeared before Cao Rui at a hunt on Mt. Dashi. As an officer of Sima Yi he distinguished himself against Shu.

@9003
Courtesy name Zao'an. Fifth son of Sun Jian, by the sister of Lady Wu (Queen Mother Wu). After his father's death he fled to Jiangnan. He later became Colonel of Martial Might in Wu.

@9004
A Shu general. Learning that his colleague Zhang Ren had been captured, he surrendered to Liu Bei. He helped Liu Bei enter Shu and went with Zhang Fei to defend the Baxi region.

@9005
Younger brother of Zhang Lu, founder of the Five Pecks of Rice sect. He argued for resisting Cao Cao to the end. He fled to Bazhong to show his will to resist, fought Cao Cao's general Xu Chu, but could not win and was cut down.

@9006
Chief of the mountain bandits called the Black Mountain. After Cao Cao defeated Yuan Tan, he surrendered with 100,000 troops. For this he was made General Who Pacifies the North and ordered to strengthen the northern strongholds.

@9007
A general under Liu Yao, Inspector of Yangzhou. He guarded Niuzhu but lost to Sun Ce. Later at Moling (Jianye) he fought Sun Ce's officer Chen Wu and was speared to death.

@9008
One of Han Sui's "Eight Banner Riders." He took Chang'an with Ma Chao. He was killed in the melee at Weinan.

@9009
Courtesy name Huishu. Colonel of Wu. As envoy returning the courtesy of the Wu-Shu alliance, he went to Shu with Deng Zhi and carried out the great task. His praise of Shu's government displeased Sun Quan, who confined him.

@9010
Courtesy name Maoxian. Secretary of Jin. While playing go with Sima Yan he backed the memorial from Du Yu urging the attack on Wu. He opposed Jia Chong's cautious view and earned Jia Chong's grudge.

@9011
A Yellow Turban. After the rebellion he served Tao Qian as a Commandant. Ordered to escort Cao Cao's father Cao Song, he was blinded by his treasure, murdered Cao Song and fled with it.

@9012
Commander under Yuan Shu. When Yuan Shu falsely claimed the throne, he was made General-in-Chief. He led 200,000 men against Lu Bu but was routed.

@9013
A Wei general and son of the famed Zhang Liao. He raided the Shu army at Mt. Qi at night and lost. Later he led archers and shot the Shu general Wu Ban dead. He also distinguished himself against the rebel Gongsun Yuan.

@9014
A Yellow Turban. He held Wancheng with Han Zhong and Sun Zhong. He fought the imperial army under Zhu Jun, but Sun Jian came to its aid, threw him from his horse, and he was killed.

@9015
General of Chariots and Cavalry under Dong Zhuo and uncle of Zhang Xiu. His wife was the beauty Lady Zou. To fix a grain shortage he attacked Liu Biao in Nanyang, but was killed by a stray arrow.

@9016
A Wu general. He tried to fight the Jin army with the navy, but a sudden wind blew all the flags upside down, and his soldiers fled in fear. Zhang Xiang had no choice but to surrender to Jin.

@9017
Palace Attendant of Shu. Second son of the fierce Zhang Fei. When Shu surrendered he took the imperial seal to Deng Ai with Qiao Zhou. After Zhong Hui's death he followed Liu Shan to Luoyang and was made a Marquis.

@9018
A Yellow Gate Attendant of Wei. When Sima Yan pressed the Wei emperor Cao Huan to abdicate, he opposed it fiercely. This angered Sima Yan, and Sima Yan's soldiers beat him to death with clubs.

@9019
A subordinate general of Zhang Fei. Ordered to prepare white mourning garb to avenge Guan Yu, he asked for more time and was refused, so, fearing for his life, he assassinated Zhang Fei with his colleague Fan Jiang.

@9020
General of the Left of Wu. On Emperor Sun Xiu's orders he executed the tyrannical Chancellor Sun Chen with the old general Ding Feng. He backed Sun Hao as successor, but was killed with his clan after remonstrating against Sun Hao's cruelty.

@9021
A follower of Guan Yu, unmatched in loyalty. He devised plans as adviser during Guan Yu's retreat, accompanied him out of Maicheng, ran into a Wu ambush, and was killed in battle.

@9022
A general under Liu Yao, Inspector of Yangzhou. He guarded Moling (Jianye) with Xue Li, Zhang Ying and others. He was shot dead by an arrow from Jiang Qin, an officer of Sun Ce, who had invaded Yangzhou.

@9023
General Who Pacifies the East of Wei. In Zhuge Dan's rebellion he was deputy vanguard of the suppression force and beat the Wu army aiding Zhuge Dan. When Sima Yan became King of Jin he was made General of Chariots and Cavalry.

@9024
A Shu general, said to be the father of Chen Shou. Captured at Mt. Dingjun, he was exchanged for the Wei general Xiahou Shang. In the third Northern Expedition he failed to pursue the Wei army and was beheaded for the defeat.

@9025
Courtesy name Kongzhang. One of the "Seven Scholars of Jian'an." Yuan Shao took him on as a secretary and he wrote the manifesto calling for Cao Cao's death. It was so well done that even Cao Cao praised it.

@9026
One of Han Sui's "Eight Banner Riders." He took Chang'an with Ma Chao's army. Later he was killed in the melee at Weinan with his colleague Zhang Heng and others.

@9027
A Wei official, son of the Wei strategist Cheng Yu. He followed Xiahou Mao as army adviser. Facing Zhao Yun, he advised Xiahou Mao to beat him with an ambush, driving Zhao Yun into a corner, if only for a time.

@9028
Courtesy name Dushu. A staff officer of Sun Quan. When Liu Bei attacked to avenge Guan Yu, he went as envoy for peace and delivered Zhang Fei's head. Liu Bei's resolve was firm and the peace failed.

@9029
A Wei general, great-nephew of Tian Chou. He joined Zhong Hui and Deng Ai's campaign against Shu. Holding a grudge against Deng Ai, he used Zhong Hui's revolt to cut down Deng Ai and his son Deng Zhong, in custody at Mianzhu Pass.

@9030
Courtesy name Zitai. A general under Yuan Shao. Cao Cao, looking for someone who knew the land, summoned him to guide the way to the Wuhuan. For this and his loyalty to the Yuans he was made Counselor.

@9031
A Wei official and staff officer of Deng Ai. To make Jiang Wei's army fall back, he bribed the Shu eunuch Huang Hao with gold and silver to spread a rumor of Jiang Wei's treason. The plan worked, and he reported to Deng Ai at Mt. Qi.

@9032
Courtesy name Gongxi. A Shu official. He followed Zhuge Liang on the southern and northern campaigns. Against Wei's invasion he defended Jiange with Jiang Wei. After Shu fell he fell ill from heartache and died.

@9033
A general under Liu Zhang. He visited the hermit Zixu with Liu Gui to have the future of Shu and of himself told. He could not make use of the prophecy and was killed by Huang Zhong's arrow.

@9034
A Wei general. He led the Seven Armies of Wei to the relief of Cao Ren with Yu Jin and Pang De. When the enemy general Guan Yu's flood swamped the camp, he urged surrender, and was cut down by the angry Pang De.

@9035
A Wu general. He was sent as relief when Zhuge Dan rebelled against the Simas. Defeated and captured, he surrendered to Wei and was later well employed in the Wei army.

@9036
A Wu general. He tried to meet Wang Jun's Jin advance with the navy, but in one night all 20,000 of his men fled in fear of an ill omen, and his ally Zhang Xiang also surrendered. He lost without a fight.

@9037
General of Chariots and Cavalry of the Later Han. As Emperor Xian's maternal uncle he was called the "Uncle of the State." The emperor gave him a jade belt with the order to kill Cao Cao. A servant informed on his plot and he was executed.

@9038
A Wei general and son of Deng Ai. With his father he beat Jiang Wei at Duangu. He joined the campaign against Shu, but Zhong Hui's scheme made his father suspected of treason, and he was sent back to Luoyang with him.

@9039
Younger brother of Dong Zhuo. He became General of the Left as his brother rose. When Dong Zhuo was killed through Lu Bu's betrayal, he was seized as an accomplice, beheaded and his head displayed.

@9040
A Yellow Turban. As deputy to Cheng Yuanzhi he raided Youzhou with him, and was felled in one exchange by Zhang Fei, who had joined the Youzhou volunteer army.

@9041
Courtesy name Youzai. A retainer of Liu Zhang. To stop Liu Bei's army he advised asking Zhang Lu for help. After Liu Bei entered Shu he was made Colonel Who Commands the Army.

@9042
A bandit. When Guan Yu left Cao Cao to rejoin Liu Bei, he kidnapped Liu Bei's two wives. His comrade Liao Hua objected, so Du Yuan was cut down and the two ladies were freed.

@9043
Gentleman Cavalier Attendant of Wei. When Sima Zhao, thinking of making his second son Sima You his heir, asked his opinion, he opposed it with Jia Chong. Later he helped Sima Yan take the throne.

@9044
A Shu general under Zhang Fei. Unable to prepare the white garb, he asked for more time and was refused, so he assassinated Zhang Fei with his colleague Zhang Da and fled to Wu. Later Sun Quan sent him to Shu, where he was killed.

@9045
Courtesy name Changyuan. A Shu official who took part in the southern and northern campaigns as a civil officer. After Shu fell he was sent to Luoyang with Liu Shan and others. Sima Zhao made him a marquis.

@9046
Administrator of Jingzhou. He had talent for domestic affairs, but Wang Fu called him "suspicious, moved by profit and untrustworthy." As feared, he made no effort to stop Mi Fang and the others from surrendering.

@9047
A general under Dong Zhuo. After Dong Zhuo's death he drove back the army of Ma Teng and Han Sui and pursued it. Because he spared Han Sui as a fellow countryman, Li Jue had him cut down for treason.

@9048
A general under Han Fu, Inspector of Jizhou. At the Battle of Sishui Pass he challenged the enemy general Hua Xiong to a duel with a great ax, but lacked the strength and was cut down.

@9049
A general under Han Sui and one of the "Eight Banner Riders." When Ma Chao, taken in by Jia Xu's scheme of discord, attacked, he tried to protect his lord Han Sui but was felled in an instant.
