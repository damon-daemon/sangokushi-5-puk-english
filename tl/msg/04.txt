@4000
Courtesy name Zhongxuan. A retainer of Liu Biao. After Liu Biao's death he urged Liu Cong, with Kuai Yue, to surrender to Cao Cao and was later made Marquis Within the Pass. A fine writer, one of the "Seven Scholars of Jian'an."

@4001
Courtesy name Zijun. A Wei general disliked by his superior Xu Huang in the war on Shu, he defected to Shu. He served in the Southern and Northern campaigns and at Jieting, as Ma Su's deputy, fought well with few troops.

@4002
Courtesy name Yigong. A veteran who served three generations of the Sun family. He fought in the Yangzhou campaign, Chibi, the war on Guan Yu and Yiling, and formed a wing of Wu's navy.

@4003
A general under Cao Ren. In the war on Wu he lured Gan Ning into Yiling and besieged him, but Zhou Yu's relief army broke the siege. His night raid on Zhou Yu's camp was seen through and he was crushed.

@4004
Under Yuan Shu, a wielder of the "three-pointed blade." When Liu Bei's army attacked he traded thirty bouts with the famed Guan Yu. He later attacked Liu Bei on Yuan Shu's order but withdrew at Lu Bu's mediation.

@4005
Courtesy name Gongheng. He joined Liu Bei after the entry into Shu. In the war on Wu he led the navy, but was cut off when the main army lost and reluctantly surrendered to Wei. Even then his loyalty to Liu Bei never changed.

@4006
Courtesy name Gongda. Nephew of Xun Yu, who served Cao Cao with him. Skilled in military affairs, he gave much advice on the field. At Chibi he served as staff officer with Cheng Yu.

@4007
A retainer of Han Fu. After Han Fu fled he served the Yuans and was a staff officer of Yuan Tan. Meeting Cao Cao as an envoy, he was invited to join him but firmly declined.

@4008
Courtesy name Gongyou. Recommended by Tao Qian as Liu Bei's aide. Adviser and diplomat who helped Liu Bei in his wandering years. After Liu Bei entered Shu he was treated second only to Mi Zhu.

@4009
Nephew of Zhang Ji. After Zhang Ji's death he led his army and aided Liu Biao. He opposed Cao Cao and kept winning thanks to his adviser Jia Xu. On Jia Xu's advice he later submitted to Cao Cao and was given high favor.

@4010
Courtesy name Demou. Wielder of the "iron-spined serpent spear." He served three generations of the Suns. At Chibi he cooperated as deputy commander with commander Zhou Yu and beat Cao Cao's great army.

@4011
Courtesy name Zizhong. From a wealthy merchant family, he was Tao Qian's chief clerk. After Tao Qian's death he aided Liu Bei. After the entry into Shu he became General Who Pacifies Han, ranked above Zhuge Liang.

@4012
Courtesy name Zifang. Younger brother of Mi Zhu. He guarded Nanjun in Jingzhou under Guan Yu, but persuaded by his colleague Fu Shiren he betrayed Guan Yu and went over to Wu. Later he returned to Shu, was not forgiven, and was executed.

@4013
Courtesy name Xiuzhao. Shu's Attendant Gentleman. It is said that while he lived even Huang Hao could not act freely. Kongming's "Memorial on Deploying the Army" for the Northern Expedition also names him a loyal minister.

@4014
Real name Wen Shu. Wen Yang was his common name from his childhood name A Yang. Son of Wen Qin. A fierce general compared to Zhao Yun, he joined Guanqiu Jian's revolt and troubled Sima Shi. After his father's death he went over to Wei.

@4015
Courtesy name Boning. Recommended by Liu Ye, he served Cao Cao. He urged Cao Ren, who meant to abandon Fancheng, to hold it, and won. He also won a great victory over the Wu army that answered Shu's Northern Expedition.

@4016
Courtesy name Mancheng. A Wei general and veteran who followed Cao Cao since the Yellow Turban days. Calm and careful, he often restrained his colleagues' rashness. He defended Hefei with Zhang Liao and Yue Jin.

@4017
Courtesy name Ziyang. Recommended by Guo Jia, he served Cao Cao. Against Yuan Shao he devised the catapult to repel enemy attacks. He served three Cao generations with many proposals and became a Counselor of Wei.

@4018
Nephew of Yuan Shu. After Yuan Shu's death he fled to Lujiang guarding his coffin and family, and they were all slaughtered there. The imperial seal was taken and presented to Cao Cao.

@4019
Served Zhang Lu as adviser, giving much advice. He dissuaded Zhang Lu, who believed Yang Song's slander, from killing Pang De. After Zhang Lu surrendered, Cao Cao made him a marquis.

@4020
Courtesy name Zishi. Minister over the Masses of Han. To kill the tyrant Dong Zhuo he carried out the "Chain Plan" using the beauty Diao Chan. After Dong Zhuo's death he could not control the rebellious remnants under Li Jue and was killed.

@4021
Courtesy name Zhongwen (Zhonggong). A Wei general who opposed Sima Shi's deposition of Cao Fang and rebelled with Wen Qin. Beaten by Sima Shi's army, he turned to Song Bai, magistrate of Shen, but was tricked and killed.

@4022
Courtesy name Derun. A retainer of Sun Quan. He helped Huang Gai's "bitter flesh plan," going to Cao Cao's camp as envoy himself and making it succeed. Against Liu Bei's advance he strongly recommended Lu Xun.

@4023
Courtesy name Xianhe. Liu Bei's adviser from the same hometown, who followed him from the first uprising as envoy and staff officer. After the entry into Shu he was made General of Illustrious Virtue. Seemingly indifferent to others' opinion of him.

@4024
Eldest son of Liu Zhang. He asked his father to let him meet Liu Bei's army marching on Chengdu but lost. According to the histories he held off Liu Bei's main army for nearly a year.

@4025
Courtesy name Wenxiu. When Liu Bei urged Liu Zhang to surrender, he submitted at once. His reputation in judging people equaled his cousin Xu Zijiang's. When Liu Bei became emperor he was Minister over the Masses of Shu-Han.

@4026
Courtesy name Ziyuan. Adviser to Yuan Shao. At Guandu he went over to his old friend Cao Cao and told him about Wuchao. Cao Cao's army, near defeat, won a great victory by surprise attack on that information.

@4027
Courtesy name Zhongxiang. A Kuaiji clerk. After Governor Wang Lang lost to Sun Ce he served Sun Ce. For the badly wounded Zhou Tai he recommended the famed doctor Hua Tuo. He later succeeded in turning Fu Shiren.

@4028
A Liu Biao general and defender of Jiangxia. He treated his man Gan Ning coldly for his pirate origins, so Gan Ning defected to Wu. He was later cut down by Gan Ning, by then a Sun Quan general.

@4029
Courtesy name Yuanxiong. A Shu general related to Wu Yi. In the war on Wu he won the first battles and besieged the enemy general Sun Huan at Yiling. In the Northern Expedition he was shot dead by the Wei generals Zhang Hu and Yue Chen.

@4030
Courtesy name Yuantan. A student of Cai Yong. Recommended by Zhang Hong, he served Sun Quan. Quiet, he did not drink. When Sun Quan took the throne he was made Chancellor.

@4031
A Shu general who joined Liu Bei after the entry into Shu and fought Wei under Ma Chao. At Xiegu he dueled Cao Cao's son Cao Zhang but was too weak and was cut down.

@4032
A Wu general, third son of Sun Shao. At Shu's request he went with the old general Ding Feng to relieve Zhuge Zhan but was too late. He was later promoted to General Who Commands the Army.

@4033
A Shu general, nephew of Xiang Lang. In the "Memorial on Deploying the Army" before the Northern Expedition, Zhuge Liang praised him as fair in nature and conduct and versed in military matters, and put him in charge of rear defense.

@4034
Courtesy name Siyuan. Son of Zhuge Liang. At seventeen he married a daughter of Liu Shan. At Mianzhu Pass he beat Deng Zhong and Shi Zuan, then charged ahead without waiting for reinforcements, was surrounded, and killed himself.

@4035
Courtesy name Zuozhi. Younger brother of Xin Ping. He aided Yuan Tan, and after the Yuans fell served Cao Cao, who trusted him. At Wuzhangyuan he delivered Cao Rui's edict to Sima Yi, facing Zhuge Liang, and held back the eager army.

@4036
Courtesy name Zixiu. Eldest son of Cao Cao. When Zhang Xiu attacked at night and Cao Cao lost his horse, he gave him his own. Cao Cao survived but he could not escape and fell in battle.

@4037
Courtesy name Zilie. Sixth son of Sun Quan. Raised to emperor after Sun Chen deposed Sun Liang. Sun Chen later grew tyrannical and he plotted to seize power, killing Sun Chen with Ding Feng's help.

@4038
Courtesy name Chengyuan. A Wu general. With Zhang Bu he killed Sun Chen and was promoted to General-in-Chief. He led relief to Shu but was too late and Shu fell. Later he guarded the Yangtze area.

@4039
Courtesy name Bomiao. He shuttled between Shu and Wu as envoy to forge their alliance, and succeeded. In the Northern Expedition he led an army as vanguard with Zhao Yun. A general of wit and valor.

@4040
Courtesy name Yuankai. Jin's General of the Right. A lover of the "Zuo Commentary on the Spring and Autumn Annals," he called himself addicted to it. In the war on Wu he fought as General Who Pacifies the South and beat the Wu army.

@4041
Courtesy name Shuzi. Jin's Commander. He held the front line against Wu at Xiangyang, working to calm the people and waiting for disorder in the Wu army. He shared a friendship beyond enemy lines with Wu's famed Lu Kang.

@4042
Second son of Liu Biao. Backed by Cai Mao and others, he became Liu Biao's heir. He surrendered to Cao Cao's southern advance and was made Inspector of Qingzhou, but was assassinated by Cao Cao's men on the way to Qingzhou.

@4043
Adopted son of Liu Bei. While guarding Shangyong he refused to help Guan Yu, who asked for aid. Guan Yu died, and the enraged Liu Bei executed him in Chengdu.

@4044
Courtesy name Ziyu. Wei's Director of the Secretariat. Once a Han minister, he changed masters from Yuan Shu to Sun Ce to Cao Cao. He threatened Emperor Xian into yielding to Cao Pi. History and the novel judge him very differently.

@4045
Courtesy name Liangdao. Wei's Counselor Remonstrant. He talked Cao Zhang out of contending with Cao Pi for the succession. In the war on Wu, as Cao Xiu's staff officer, he alone saw through Zhou Fang's false surrender.

@4046
Courtesy name Lingxian. Shu's Secretary. He handled domestic affairs when Zhuge Liang left for the Northern Expedition. After Shu fell he was sent to Luoyang with Liu Shan. He is famous for rebuking Liu Shan for frolicking at a banquet.

@4047
Courtesy name Yuanchang. He defended Chang'an but lost it to Ma Chao's fierce attack. After Cao Rui took the throne he was made Grand Tutor. Zhong Hui was his youngest son.

@4048
Courtesy name Xuanbo. Son of Chen Qun and Wei's Director of the Secretariat. Said to command armies better than his father. When Cao Mao died he came to court in mourning dress, fulfilling his loyalty as a Wei minister.

@4049
Courtesy name Zigan. A Han minister and Liu Bei's teacher in his youth. As a colonel he led the army against the Yellow Turbans with merit. Later, as a secretary, he opposed Dong Zhuo's deposing of the young emperor.
