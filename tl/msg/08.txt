Courtesy name Jizhong. Magistrate of Chaoge. Invited by Cao Pi as an adviser, he went to his residence hidden in a trunk of silks. When this was reported, he next had a trunk carried in with only silk.

@8001
Cousin of Cai Mao. Just before Chibi he surrendered to Wu with Cai Zhong. Zhou Yu saw through the sham surrender and used the "Self-Injury Ruse," so he sent false information.

@8002
Cousin of Cai Mao. He surrendered to Wu with Cai He as a "poisoned plant," but was seen through. After guiding Wu through Cao Cao's camp, he was cut down by the Wu general Gan Ning.

@8003
At first he served Tao Qian, but embezzled goods in transit and built a Buddhist temple. Later, as a general under Liu Yao, he fought Sun Ce and lost, and fled with Liu Yao.

@8004
A Wu general. At the Battle of Yiling he fought the Shu army under Sun Huan. He could not beat Zhang Bao, and escaped once, but the next day Zhang Bao attacked again and speared him to death.

@8005
General of Chariots and Cavalry of the Later Han. After Lu Bu fell he governed Xuzhou for Liu Bei, who had gone to Xuchang. When Liu Bei returned he plotted to assassinate him on Cao Cao's order, but Guan Yu saw through it and killed him.

@8006
A Wu general. During Zhuge Dan's revolt he was vanguard of the relief force on the orders of Chancellor Sun Chen. He fought Zhong Hui's army, but kept losing and angered Sun Chen. He then lost to Sima Zhao and was finally executed by Sun Chen.

@8007
A retainer of Wang Lang, Governor of Kuaiji. He went to help Wang Lang fight the enemy general Taishi Ci, but was attacked from behind and fled into the city. He pursued with guest general Yan Baihu, fell into Sun Ce's trap and was cut down.

@8008
A Jin Lieutenant under Du Yu, who joined the campaign against Wu. Leading 800 marines, he took Xiakou and killed the enemy generals Sun Xin and Shen Ying, a great feat.

@8009
Inspector of Yanzhou for Wei. He joined the campaign against Zhuge Dan as the right flank. On Zhong Hui's plan he lured the enemy out, and with Shi Bao led an ambush to win.

@8010
Courtesy name Ziyu. Governor of Poyang in Wu. He cut off his hair to win Cao Xiu's trust in his surrender, and guided Cao Xiu's army to Shiting, then vanished. The surrender was a ruse, and the Wei army was attacked and badly beaten.

@8011
Courtesy name Yifeng. A Wu general who led the navy at the Battle of Yiling. He pursued Liu Bei, routed by Lu Xun's fire attack, but ran into Zhao Yun's relief force and was beaten.

@8012
Courtesy name Junli. A veteran who served three generations of the Suns. He advised Sun Ce to borrow troops from Yuan Shu and become independent. Later Governor of Wu Commandery, he was Patrol Inspector of the Four Quarters at Chibi.

@8013
A general under Cao Cao. He accompanied Liu Bei as army supervisor when Liu Bei went to attack Yuan Shu, but failed to keep watch and returned to camp. The official history says Cao Cao always disliked him.

@8014
Courtesy name Ziyi. A staff officer of Cao Cao. He went to persuade his old friend Zhou Yu of Wu but was used instead. In the official history he went to persuade, was moved by his friend Zhou Yu's loyalty, and returned silent.

@8015
A general under Yuan Shao. He met Yuan Shao at Liyang after his defeat by Cao Cao at Guandu. He rallied the scattered troops and brought Yuan Shao safely back to Jizhou.

@8016
Courtesy name Zitong. Registrar of Wei. He remonstrated when Cao Cao, fearing Guan Yu's advance, considered moving the capital. A strategist in the Wu campaign, later Grand Commandant. Said to be a good judge of people.

@8017
A Shu general. On Jiang Wei's orders for the campaign against Wei, he guarded Yangping Pass with Fu Qian. When Zhong Hui's army attacked, he surrendered and gave up the pass. His colleague Fu Qian died in the battle.

@8018
A retainer of Liu Bei. He submitted to Liu Bei after Liu Biao's death. He assisted Guan Yu in defending Jingzhou. Uncle of Xiang Chong.

@8019
Courtesy name Zhongsi. Son of Zhuge Dan. Sent to Wu as a hostage, he stayed and served Wu. As General of the Right he met the Jin invasion but was beaten. His son Zhuge Hui served Jin.

@8020
A Wei Colonel before the Tent. He followed Deng Ai in the campaign against Shu. At Mianzhu he was startled by the "wooden statue of Zhuge Liang" the enemy general Zhuge Zhan sent out, and was wounded.

@8021
Courtesy name Shuda. Younger brother of Sima Yi and Grand Tutor of Wei. He kept to his place as a subject of Wei. He wept at Cao Mao's death and served the deposed Wei emperor Cao Huan even after Sima Yan usurped the throne.

@8022
Courtesy name Zichu. Son of Sima Fu, he became the adopted son of Sima Lang. He fought Shu mostly as Deng Ai's adviser. When Sima Yan became king he was named Minister of Education.

@8023
Courtesy name Boda. Elder brother of Sima Yi, he served as Registrar. The Sima brothers were all excellent, called the "Eight Da of Sima." The official history ranks him with Xun Shuang as a byword for a noted scholar.

@8024
General of the Left of Wu. Though outnumbered, he resisted with Zhang Ti, commanding the Wu army. He urged Zhang Ti to surrender and was refused, but fought on at his side to the end and was killed by the Jin general Zhou Zhi.

@8025
Governor of Shangyong in Wei. He went over to Shu after Cao Cao gave up Hanzhong. He urged Meng Da, whose position had worsened, to surrender to Wei. Later he attacked Meng Da when Meng Da tried to defect to Shu again.

@8026
A retainer of Liu Zhang. He rebuked the arrogance of Liu Bei's envoy Jian Yong, and joined Liu Bei after he entered Shu. He was jailed for opposing Liu Bei's campaign against Wu. Later he out-argued the Wu envoy Zhang Wen at a banquet.

@8027
A Wei General Who Supports the State, a master of the great ax whose valor Sima Shi praised. He fought and drove back Liao Hua and Zhang Yi. At Mt. Tielong he fought Jiang Wei and lost to his spear.

@8028
One of Han Sui's "Eight Banner Riders." He took Chang'an with Ma Chao, but was lured to an empty enemy camp, surrounded, and cut down by Xiahou Yuan.

@8029
Courtesy name Daoyan. Son of Xue Zong and Superintendent of the Household of Wu. When Jin advanced and Sun Hao meant to kill himself, he advised him to surrender, following the precedent of Liu Shan.

@8030
Courtesy name Jingwen. Invited by Sun Quan to be his aide. A master of civil government and good at drafting documents, he later became Junior Tutor to the Crown Prince. In the Romance he debates Zhuge Liang.

@8031
A Wu general, son of Quan Duan and grandson of Quan Cong. He came to the aid of Zhuge Dan's rebellion against Wei. Surrounded by Sima Zhao's great Wei army, he had to surrender and was made a Wei General of the Flank.

@8032
A Wu general, son of Quan Cong. He helped Zhuge Dan's rebellion against Wei and defended Shouchun with him. At his nephew's urging he opened the gates of Shouchun and surrendered to Wei.

@8033
A Wu general, son of Quan Cong and elder brother of Quan Yi. Sun Chen, answering Zhuge Dan's rebellion, ordered him to defend Shouchun, but on his son's advice he surrendered to Wei with Quan Yi.

@8034
Courtesy name Pengzu. Prince of Yan in Wei, a son of Cao Pi (of Cao Cao in the official history). Cao Rui ordered him to become General-in-Chief and assist Cao Fang, but the gentle man firmly declined the great task.

@8035
Second son of Cao Zhen and younger brother of Cao Shuang. He warned his brother, who had taken military power from Sima Yi and grown arrogant, but was not heard. Later he was executed with his brother.

@8036
Third son of Cao Zhen. His brother Cao Shuang took military power from Sima Yi and named him General of the Martial Guard. Later Sima Yi staged a coup, seized military power and had him executed with Cao Shuang.

@8037
A general under Lu Bu. Seeing his colleague Hou Cheng beaten with rods for a trifle, he surrendered to Cao Cao with Hou Cheng and Wei Xu. Later, in the war with Yuan Shao, he was cut down by the fierce Yan Liang.

@8038
Courtesy name Zihe. Younger brother of Cao Ren. He led the "Tiger Leopard Cavalry," Cao Cao's elite corps. He marched to relieve Cao Ren, who was defending Nan Commandery, but was beaten by the Wu general Zhou Yu.

@8039
A general under Lu Bu. Seeing his colleague Gao Shun chased by the enemy general Xiahou Dun, he shot an arrow from camp that hit Xiahou Dun's left eye. Xiahou Dun, enraged, speared him to death at once.

@8040
Son of Yuan Shao's adviser Ju Shou. He served Yuan Shang and defended Handan. When the enemy general Zhang Liao attacked, he sallied and crossed blades with him, but could not win, and was shot with an arrow as he fled.

@8041
A general under Huang Zu and Military Commander of Jiangxia. Concerned that Gan Ning, a former bandit, was not given important posts, he let him move to another country. Later captured fighting Sun Quan, he was spared by Gan Ning.

@8042
Courtesy name Darong. In the campaign against Dong Zhuo, he put on Sun Jian's red headscarf as a decoy and let Sun Jian escape from Hua Xiong's encirclement. He himself was cut down by Hua Xiong.

@8043
Courtesy name Jizuo. Fourth son of Sun Jian. After his father's death he fled to Jiangnan. Cao Cao sought friendship with Sun Ce and betrothed him to a daughter of Cao Ren (a brother of Cao Cao in the official history).

@8044
General of Agile Cavalry of Wu. When Jin invaded Wu he led an army to Xiakou, but was hit by a night raid from Du Yu's officer Zhou Zhi. Crying, "Did the Jin army fly across the Yangtze?" he was cut down by Zhou Zhi.

@8045
Courtesy name Ziyuan. Great-grandson of Sun Jing. Zhuge Ke took command of the Imperial Guard from him. He joined Teng Yin and killed the tyrannical Zhuge Ke at a banquet, and for this became Chancellor and General-in-Chief.

@8046
General of the Van of Wu and grandson of Sun Kuang. He guarded Xiakou but, fearing the cruel Sun Hao, defected to Jin, where he was made General of Agile Cavalry. When Wu fell he wept, "Heaven, whose sin is this?"

@8047
Courtesy name Gongli. Cousin of Sun Huan. In a council against Wei he clashed with the commander Xu Sheng. He moved his troops on his own and threw Wei into confusion, and with help from Xu Sheng and Ding Feng won a great victory.

@8048
Courtesy name Youtai. Younger brother of Sun Jian. He warned against Sun Ce's attack on Liu Biao. After his brother's death he aided his nephew Sun Ce. After beating Wang Lang he defended his home Kuaiji.

@8049
A Yellow Turban. He held Wancheng with Han Zhong and Zhao Hong. Defeated by the imperial army of Zhu Jun, Liu Bei and others, he fled, and Liu Bei shot him and he tumbled from his horse.
