@52165
The aim of this game is simple. Take all 47 cities for yourself.\nEasier said than done, mind you. Use your wits and expand your territory well. I am rooting for you.

@52166
There are three ways to expand your territory.\nFirst, move into an empty city. That is a city no one rules, one with no flag on the map. Just move one of your officers there and the city is yours.\nOther rulers' cities are not so easy. You will have to take them in war.\nThere is also "Threaten" under the Diplomacy command, which can make another ruler and all his lands your vassal. Of course, it rarely succeeds.

@52167
As your lands and retainers grow, so does what you must do, and the number of commands you can give each month will run short.\nThat number depends on your Fame. As your Fame rises, you get more commands each month.\nBy the way, the most is 10 and the least is 3.

@52168
Fame shows what people think of a ruler's actions. On screen it appears as a heart mark.\nDo praiseworthy things and it rises; do evil and it falls.\nWith more Fame you can give more commands each month, and there are many other advantages besides.\nIt is wise to keep an eye on your Fame.

@52169
There are several ways to raise Fame.\nFirst, hold the Council in January and meet the goals set there within the year. But if you fail, your Fame will fall instead.\nNext, use "Patrol" to tour your lands and do what the people wish. A ruler must be kind to his subjects.\nThen there are the events that happen now and then. How you handle them tests your true worth. Above all, always strive for benevolent rule.

@52170
The people's feelings toward you are shown by each city's "Support". High means they love you; low means they dislike you.\nPatrol is the way to raise Support. By meeting the people you can win their hearts.\nCities newly taken in war often have low Support. Patrol early and make friends with the people.\nAlso, when a disaster hits your cities, patrol at once and save the suffering people. Your reputation will rise too.

@52171
The "Council" is a meeting that sets your goals for the year. You can hold it every January.\nAt the Council, a representative of each ministry proposes various goals. Choose the ones you think you can achieve.\nAchieve a goal within the year and your Fame rises, so do not neglect the Council.\nFail, and your Fame falls. If no goal looks achievable, choosing "No goal" is one option too.

@52172
To hold the Council, at least 1 minister must be appointed in 4 of the 5 departments.\nWith that done, each January your Adjutant will say "Shall we hold the Council?" and you decide whether to hold it.

@52173
No officer, however able, can do every job alone.\nThe minister system sets what kind of work each officer does: "this one handles Domestic", "that one is the Diplomacy specialist".\nThere are 5 departments: Domestic, Personnel, Schemes, Diplomacy and Military. An officer who does a job is chosen from that department's ministers. You cannot have, say, a Domestic minister lead the Military, so take care.\nMinisters can be appointed only in January, April, July and October.

@52174
"Strength" is an officer's fighting power. An officer with high Strength can be expected to do well in war.\nMilitary commands like "Training" also give better results with high Strength.

@52175
"Intelligence" is an officer's cleverness.\nWith high Intelligence your schemes succeed more often, and you are more likely to block enemy schemes.\nAdjutants and Schemes ministers need high Intelligence.

@52176
"Politics" is the ability for steady political work such as Domestic and Diplomacy.\nAn officer with high Politics makes a reliable Domestic or Diplomacy minister.\nIt also affects whether Personnel commands like "Recruit" succeed.

@52177
It is the power to attract people. An officer with high Charm is easily liked by others.\nSuch an officer suits the post of Personnel minister.\nIt is also an important trait for a ruler, who must lead many officers and people.

@52178
It is the world's reputation for an officer's martial prowess.\n"Valor" rises mainly through deeds in war, so strong warriors with high Strength gain it more easily.\nWith more Valor an officer can become a higher-ranking general. A higher rank means more troops he can lead, so give strong officers plenty of battle experience.\nMore Valor also has other effects, such as gathering more troops with "Enlist".

@52179
"Experience" shows how much an officer has experienced so far.\nIt rises as he carries out his work.\nWith more Experience he can learn new formations and acquire skills.

@52180
Fine warriors must be given higher posts and the authority to lead large armies. That is what "General Rank" is for.\nThe higher an officer's rank, the more troops he can field. Appoint your retainers to high ranks and your army's strength grows.\nBut if you suddenly make an officer with no record a general, no one will accept it. To raise rank an officer needs a certain amount of "Valor".\nLet able officers win glory in real battles and raise their Valor.

@52181
It shows how much loyalty an officer holds for the ruler he serves.\nAn officer with low "Loyalty" may easily turn coat at an enemy's invitation, or rebel and try to seize your land.\nLoyalty can be raised with a "Reward" or by "Presenting" an item.

@52182
"Spirit" is an officer's drive.\nOfficers are only human. However able, if they work without rest they tire. When their Spirit falls from fatigue, they cannot work.\nSpirit recovers a little each month. Give an officer with low Spirit no work and let him rest, and he will soon be well.\nA "Feast" restores it all at once.\nA ruler's duty is not only to work his officers but sometimes to let them rest.

@52183
These are the special techniques each officer has. They work mainly on the battlefield. For example, shooting arrows farther than other officers or using powerful schemes; all are things that give an edge in war.\nAs an officer gains Experience, he learns new skills.\nHowever, one officer can learn at most 6 skills. Which ones he learns differs by officer.\nEven an officer who seems unpromising may hide a surprisingly powerful skill.

@52184
It shows a city's food production. The higher the "Farming Value", the more food you get in July.\nFarming Value rises when you do "Domestic" in that city. How high it can go differs by city.

@52185
It shows how far a city's commerce has developed. The higher the "Commerce", the more gold you get each season.\nCommerce rises when you do "Domestic" in that city. How high it can go differs by city.

@52186
It shows how well a city is prepared against floods. When a disaster like a flood strikes, high Flood Control means less damage.\nFlood Control rises when you do "Domestic" in that city. The limit is 100.

@52187
It shows the strength of a city's walls and gates when it becomes a battlefield.\nWith high Defense, units holding the castle gain an advantage and can resist stubbornly.\nDefense can be raised with "Domestic". The limit is 999.

@52188
The number of people living in a city. With a large population, gold and food income grow and many troops can be hired.\nPopulation grows naturally every January, but falls with "Conscript" and "Enlist". Mind the population when hiring troops.

@52189
It shows the feelings the city's people hold toward their ruler. It is an important sign of whether the ruler governs justly.\nWith high Support, the people cooperate and good effects follow, such as doubling the effect of "Domestic".\nWith low Support, enemy schemes like "Rumor" work easily, and in bad cases the people revolt.\nTo raise Support, patrol often and grant the people's wishes.\nTroubling the people with "Conscript" or "Plunder" will quickly lower Support.

@52190
The total troops led by the officers in a city. It grows when you assign troops gathered by "Conscript" or "Enlist" to officers.

@52191
Troops not assigned to any officer. Spare troops cannot fight. Assign them to officers with "Organize" as soon as you can.

@52192
Are you trying to give an officer work outside his duty? For example, you cannot have a Diplomacy minister do schemes. In that case his name does not appear in the officer selection list.\nThe other case is when his Spirit is too low. Then his name appears in black in the list. Until his Spirit recovers, he cannot work.

@52193
A Strategist is an expert in schemes and formations, with high Intelligence and Politics.\nAn ordinary officer can hold up to 8,000 troops; to hold more he must raise his Valor and become a general. A Strategist can hold 20,000 from the start.\nAlso, in war an ordinary officer uses up one action to change formation, but a Strategist can still move or attack after changing it.

@52194
What a Strategist needs is Intelligence and Politics. An officer with Intelligence 90 or more and Politics 80 or more may call himself a Strategist.\nNote that raising Intelligence or Politics with items does not make you a Strategist. It is easy to mistake, so take care.

@52195
An Adjutant gives proper advice at the ruler's side. It is easily confused with Strategist, but you need not be a Strategist to be Adjutant.\nStill, the advice is only as accurate as the Adjutant's Intelligence, so appoint a Strategist-class officer. A dim Adjutant may give wrong advice and be more hindrance than help.\nChoose one with "Appoint" under "Retainers".

@52196
A Governor is a post that rules a city with no ruler in the ruler's place.\nNo special ability is needed, but appoint an officer with high Loyalty so he does not rebel.

@52197
People tend to think a high-Politics officer suits a Domestic minister, but all of an officer's abilities affect "Domestic".\nPolitics, Intelligence, Charm and Strength affect the gain in Farming Value, Flood Control, Commerce and Defense respectively, so officers strong all around suit the post.\nAlso, "Domestic" is done by all the city's Domestic ministers, so numbers can make up for weak individuals. Making every idle officer a Domestic minister is one option.

@52198
Gold income depends on the Commerce of the cities you own. Population and Support matter too.\nYour officers are paid salaries in gold automatically. More officers means more expense.\nDomestic work is essential to raise income. And as officers increase, you must invade enemy lands or move into empty cities to expand your territory.

@52199
Food income depends on the Farming Value of your lands. Population and Support matter somewhat too.\nIn autumn, food is paid out automatically to feed your troops, so if you have too many troops your food income shrinks.\nTo raise food income, raise Farming Value with "Domestic" and expand your territory with "War" and "Move" into empty cities.

@52200
When you do "Domestic", in some cities the people are uncooperative and the effect is halved.\nThat is because the people of that city do not like you. Its Support must be low. Patrol at once and meet the people. Steady effort will move their hearts.

@52201
Gold is collected as tax at the turn of the seasons, that is, January, April, July and October. January's tax is the largest. The other months are not big enough to count on.\nRemember "gold comes in January" and you will not go wrong.

@52202
Unlike gold, which comes four times a year, food comes only in July.\nWithout food you cannot stand against an enemy attack. Keep an eye on your food.

@52203
Is there a city in your land with no officer in it? A city with no officers yields no tax.\nAlso, with more officers and troops, more gold and food must be paid out, so income may drop for a while. But you can make it up by doing "Domestic" and raising tax income. Do not worry much.

@52204
There are 4 possible reasons you cannot do "Domestic".\nFirst, is there a Domestic minister in that city? Without one, no work can be done.\nNext, do you have enough funds? Each city needs 100 gold.\nOr the city may be fully developed, with all its city data at maximum.\nIf none of these apply, your Domestic minister may be tired. With too little Spirit he cannot work. Rest him from Domestic and send him to a "Feast".

@52205
Sadly there is no way to avoid disasters themselves.\nBut against "Flood" and "Typhoon", raising a city's Flood Control keeps the damage light.\nBetter safe than sorry.

@52206
Floods are a summer disaster. In a flooded city, Farming Value, Flood Control, Commerce, Population and Troops fall.\nRaising Flood Control lessens the damage. At Flood Control 100, there is almost none.

@52207
Typhoons are a summer disaster. In a city hit by a typhoon, Farming Value, Flood Control, Commerce, Population and Troops fall.\nRaise Flood Control with "Domestic" and the damage will be light.

@52208
Plague can strike all year round, but is most likely in winter.\nIn a plague-stricken city, Population, Troops, Commerce and Support fall. Officers in the city may also fall ill.\nWorse, plague spreads to neighboring cities in the next season. If plague breaks out in a city next to your land, be careful. It is wise to "Move" your important officers to other cities.

@52209
Famine is a disaster that strikes only in autumn, the harvest season. In a famine city the food harvest is halved.\nThe people will suffer from hunger. The mark of a good ruler is to "Patrol" at once and give them food.

@52210
Locusts are harmless insects normally, but in great swarms they bring disaster that threatens people's lives.\nA swarm can come at any time except winter.\nIn a city hit by locusts, Farming Value and Support drop sharply.\nThey may also move to neighboring cities the next season. Mere insects, but do not underestimate their fury.

@52211
What a Personnel minister needs is Politics. An officer with high Politics succeeds more easily at "Search" and "Recruit".\nIt is best to appoint two or more Personnel ministers. Officers have affinities; even a very able Personnel minister may fail to "Recruit" an officer he is ill matched with.\nWhen "Recruit" does not go well, try it with another minister. It often succeeds quite easily.

@52212
In the realm there are officers with high ambition who are still unknown and live in misfortune.\n"Search" is the command to find such officers.\nWhen a high-Politics officer searches, he is likely to find one. But only if there is a hidden officer in that city.

@52213
When you have commands to spare.\nAn officer found by Search becomes a free officer who serves no ruler. That is, he has not yet joined you. You must "Recruit" the found officer to make him your retainer.\nA free officer may move to another city the next month. To avoid losing a hard-won find, search when you have enough commands left to recruit afterward.

@52214
Free officers serve no ruler and live as wanderers.\nSometimes a very able officer is among them. When one is in your city, you will want to recruit him at once.

@52215
Do "Recruit" in a city with a free officer and he becomes your retainer.\nBut you can recruit free officers only within your own territory. If one is in another ruler's land or an empty city, you cannot recruit him.

@52216
Often a free officer who was in your land last month is gone the next.\nFree officers are wanderers, and at the turn of the month they may move to a neighboring city.\nWhen you find an able free officer, recruit him before anything else.

@52217
If you "Recruit" an officer of another ruler and succeed, he becomes your retainer.\nWhen luring away another's officer, watch his "Loyalty". The lower it is, the more easily he takes the bait. Beware: an officer with Loyalty 100 and a ruler's sons can never be lured away.\nTo lower an enemy officer's Loyalty, you can try the "Rumor" scheme.

@52218
Among another ruler's officers, those with Loyalty 100 and the ruler's blood relatives can never be recruited.\nOfficers who get on well with their ruler, and dutiful ones, are also hard to lure away even with somewhat low Loyalty.\n"Affinity" and "Duty" are not displayed, but anyone who knows the tale of the Three Kingdoms can guess.\nFree officers who can never be recruited bear you a grudge, such as one who refused you when captured after his country fell.

@52219
A ruler exists because of his retainers. If you neglect them, other rulers will lure them away and you will weep.\nTo keep retainers, watch their "Loyalty". For a retainer with low Loyalty, give gold with "Reward" under "Retainers" to raise it.\nAt Loyalty 100 he will never betray you.\nEven short of that, try to keep every retainer at 90 or more.

@52220
Giving an item to a retainer greatly raises his Loyalty.\nIn addition, depending on the item, his abilities may rise or he may gain special skills.\nFor example, a weapon raises Strength, and a famous steed lets him use the "Rush" skill.\nHowever, abilities raised by an item return to normal if he loses it.

@52221
As the name says, this command takes away items your retainers hold.\nConfiscated items become yours, but this is not recommended.\nWhen an item is confiscated, that officer's Loyalty falls greatly, and in bad cases he may even leave you.

@52222
Now and then, drinking and making merry is the best way to ease a tired heart.\nHold a "Feast" and let officers attend, and their "Spirit" is fully restored.\nOfficers worn out from constant work should be refreshed with a feast.\nBut holding a feast costs 100 gold and 500 food per attending officer.

@52223
"Training" is plain but very useful if used well.\nIf you have many retainers and some are idle, send them out for a year of training to gain experience.\nFor that year you cannot use them for work, but through the various events they meet while training, abilities may rise or they may obtain items. It is just the thing for growing officers.\nHowever, only officers with "Loyalty" 100 can be sent. Reward promising officers early to raise their Loyalty.

@52224
Raise an officer's general rank and the troops he can hold increase, strengthening your army.\nTo raise rank, choose "General" in "Appoint" under the "Retainers" command. A list of officers who can become generals appears; choose one.\nOfficers who can be promoted appear in green. Officers with low Valor, and rulers and Strategists who need no promotion, are not listed.\nWhen you choose an officer, the ranks he can reach appear, and you choose from them.

@52225
The Adjutant is a welcome presence, telling you the odds of success when you try something.\nHis advice is only as reliable as his Intelligence. If an officer smarter than your Adjutant joins you, make him the new Adjutant with "Appoint" under "Retainers".

@52226
Not displayed, but officers have temperaments. Those with similar ones get on well; those very different do not. This is called "Affinity".\nIt affects every situation involving two officers. For example, an officer who suits his ruler poorly is easy to lure away.\nIf you know the tale of the Three Kingdoms well, you can guess who suits whom.

@52227
A scheme's success rate depends on the "Intelligence" of the officer who carries it out.\nSo officers with high Intelligence suit the Schemes minister.\nPolitics and Strength matter a little, but appointing high-Intelligence officers is basically safe.

@52228
Of all schemes, "Rumor" is the one you will use most.\nIt spreads bad rumors about the ruler in an enemy city, lowering the city's Support and the Loyalty of its officers.\nOne try has little effect, so repeat it several times.

@52229
When "Rumor" succeeds, the Loyalty of an officer in the city may fall.\nBut only the officer with the lowest current Loyalty in that city is affected.\nSo if another officer is lower than your target, only his Loyalty drops. In that case, lure him away first.

@52230
When the target city's Support is very high, "Rumor" rarely succeeds.\nIt also works poorly if the ruler you target has high Fame or Charm.

@52231
"Turncoat" persuades an enemy officer to promise to defect in war. For the other ruler, it is like making an enemy within his own house.\nIn war, if an officer has been turned, use "Defect" to make him yours. The enemy loses troops and you gain them, so the effect is great.

@52232
"Turncoat" makes someone switch sides, so it hardly works on officers with high Loyalty.\nYou must first lower the target's Loyalty.\n"Rumor" is useful here.\nLower his Loyalty with "Rumor" beforehand, then use "Turncoat" at once. The chance of success rises.

@52233
"Rival Tigers" is a bold scheme that incites an enemy governor to declare independence.\nIf it succeeds, the enemy has more foes to fight, so its defenses thin out and it weakens. It seldom succeeds, but it may be fun to try.

@52234
"Rival Tigers" works easily on governors with low Loyalty.\nBut few rulers make a disloyal, "dangerous" officer governor. Before "Rival Tigers", prepare by lowering his Loyalty with "Rumor" and so on.

@52235
"Incite" stirs up the people of an enemy city to riot.\nIf it succeeds, when you attack that city you can use the "Incite" command to make the people riot, reducing enemy troops and lowering Defense.

@52236
Since you make the people riot, you can guess that it succeeds more easily where Support is low.\nThe higher the target city's Support, the lower the chance.\nLower Support beforehand with "Rumor".\nAlso, if the scheming ruler's Fame is too low, success is doubtful; the people will not trust him.

@52237
A successful "Incite" means nothing unless you act. It takes effect only when you attack the city.\nBut it lasts only 3 months. If you do not attack within 3 months, the promise lapses and your effort is wasted.

@52238
Cities with high "Defense" are hard to take. "Sabotage" lowers a city's Defense beforehand.\nIf it succeeds, the enemy castle is damaged and its Defense falls. Repeat it and the castle is left bare, making the battle easy.

@52239
