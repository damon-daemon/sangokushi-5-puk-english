@1000
Courtesy name Yide. Swore brotherhood with Liu Bei and Guan Yu in the Peach Garden to restore the Han. At Changban he held off Cao Cao's pursuit alone on horseback. One of Shu's Five Tiger Generals.

@1001
Courtesy name Yunchang. Swore brotherhood with Liu Bei and Zhang Fei in the Peach Garden to restore the Han. A famed general of peerless valor and a pillar of Liu Bei's army. Foremost of Shu's Five Tiger Generals.

@1002
Courtesy name Fengxian. A fierce general who won fame as the strongest under heaven. Skilled at archery and horsemanship, but with no sense of loyalty, he betrayed one lord after another. He was finally betrayed by his own men and killed.

@1003
Courtesy name Mengqi. Eldest son of Ma Teng, nicknamed "the Splendid Ma Chao" for his gallant looks. He raided Chang'an to avenge his father on Cao Cao but lost; later he joined Liu Bei and became a Tiger General.

@1004
Courtesy name Benchu. Head of the illustrious Yuan clan and leader of the anti-Dong Zhuo alliance. After Dong Zhuo's death he fought Cao Cao for supremacy in the Central Plains, but his indecision cost him a crushing defeat at Guandu.

@1005
Courtesy name Miaocai. Wei's General Who Conquers the West and son-in-law of Cao Cao. A master of surprise attacks, but at Mt. Dingjun he was fooled by Fa Zheng's plan and cut down by Huang Zhong in the melee.

@1006
Courtesy name Yuanrang. Cousin of Cao Cao. He lost his left eye to an arrow while fighting Lu Bu and became the one-eyed general. Cao Cao trusted him deeply, and under Cao Pi he was made General-in-Chief.

@1007
A fierce general under Dong Zhuo, nine chi tall. He boasted to Lu Bu that there was no need for him to sortie, and killed Zu Mao and several other enemy generals, but was then cut down by Guan Yu.

@1008
Courtesy name Zilong. A general of both wit and valor under Liu Bei, ranked among the Five Tiger Generals. His ride through Cao Cao's million men at Changban to protect Liu Bei's son A Dou is famous.

@1009
A fierce general under Yuan Shao, praised with Wen Chou as "unmatched in valor in all three armies." He led the vanguard at Guandu, killing Song Xian and Wei Xu and routing Xu Huang, but was slain by Guan Yu, then with Cao Cao.

@1010
A fierce general who, with Yan Liang, commanded Yuan Shao's great army in Hebei. At Guandu he went to avenge his sworn brother Yan Liang, but Guan Yu cut him down.

@1011
Courtesy name Zhongkang. A fierce general and trusted bodyguard of Cao Cao. He fought Dian Wei and Ma Chao to a draw in single combat. Despite his might he was usually dazed, so he was called the "Tiger Fool."

@1012
A fierce general nicknamed "E Lai." Cao Cao loved his valor and kept him close. When Zhang Xiu attacked by surprise, he faced the enemy alone and bare-handed, giving his life to save Cao Cao.

@1013
Courtesy name Wenyuan. A general of wit and valor, foremost of Cao Cao's Five Great Generals. At the Battle of Hefei he showed his genius, breaking a 100,000-man Wu army with only 800 men.

@1014
Courtesy name Wenchang. Liu Bei trusted him enough to make him Governor of Hanzhong. He clashed with Kongming and rebelled after Kongming's death, but was slain by Yang Yi and others, who held a secret plan.

@1015
A Shu general under Liu Zhang, known for his courage. He resisted Zhang Fei's attack boldly, but after defeat, moved by Zhang Fei's sincerity, he surrendered. He later fought splendidly as an old-timer pair with Huang Zhong.

@1016
Courtesy name Xingba. A Wu general who led a mere 100 horsemen in a night raid on Wei and was Wu's finest fighter. Sun Quan said, "Cao Cao has Zhang Liao; I have Gan Xingba."

@1017
Son of Guan Ding of Hebei and adopted son of Guan Yu. He fought at his foster father's side in many campaigns and died with Guan Yu at Maicheng. His gentle nature was said to be loved by the officers.

@1018
Courtesy name Gongfu. A veteran who had served since Sun Jian first raised troops. At the Battle of Chibi, which decided Wu's fate, his self-sacrificing scheme won the victory and gave rise to the saying "the bitter flesh plan."

@1019
Once a Yellow Turban bandit. He lived as a mountain robber, then served Guan Yu, whom he worshiped, and became his right arm. When Guan Yu died, he followed him in death, beheading himself in a grim end.

@1020
Courtesy name Ziwen. Third son of Cao Cao and a valiant fighter. He distinguished himself in the struggle for Hanzhong, but in later years was shunned by his brother Cao Pi and died in despair.

@1021
Courtesy name Ziyi. Under Liu Yao he was known for his deadly archery and his single combat with Sun Ce. Captured by Sun Ce, he was drawn to his character and worked hard to found the state of Wu.

@1022
Courtesy name Hansheng. Though old, he fought Guan Yu evenly. He later joined Liu Bei as one of the Five Tiger Generals. In China an old man still going strong is called a "Huang Zhong."

@1023
Courtesy name Zixiao. Cousin of Cao Cao, who followed him from the time he raised troops. He rallied his men and won even in adversity; allied generals marveled, "He is no human being."

@1024
Courtesy name Junyi. At first under Yuan Shao, he went over to Cao Cao with Gao Lan. Counted among the Five Great Generals, he won many feats. Even the enemy general Zhuge Kongming acknowledged his ability.

@1025
Cousin of Ma Chao, who surrendered to Liu Bei with him. He served in the Southern and Northern campaigns, and Kongming prized his loyalty and talent. After Kongming's death he crushed Wei Yan's revolt with a secret plan.

@1026
Courtesy name Shoucheng. A fierce Xiliang general, descendant of the Later Han general Ma Yuan. His part in the Emperor Xian's secret order to kill Cao Cao was found out, and he was lured to the capital and killed by Cao Cao.

@1027
Courtesy name Lingming. A trusted aide of Ma Chao. Later captured by Cao Cao, he served him well. At Fancheng he fought Guan Yu and his son evenly, but lost to Guan Yu's flood attack.

@1028
King of the Nanman. After Liu Bei's death he rebelled against Shu with about 100,000 barbarian troops, but submitted after Kongming's "seven captures." He was then entrusted with the south and defended it himself.

@1029
Courtesy name Ziming. Once only a crude fighter, he was urged by Sun Quan to study and grew into a general of wit and valor. With Lu Xun, whom he recommended, he skillfully coordinated to defeat Guan Yu.

@1030
Courtesy name Jibo. Once a guest advisor of Liu Biao, he later served Liu Bei and compiled the "Shu Code." Full of wit, he was an orator whose eloquence impressed even Sun Quan.

@1031
Courtesy name Gonglu. Half-brother of Yuan Shao and Governor of Nanyang. He took the imperial seal and proclaimed himself emperor, but was abandoned by people and soldiers alike and came to a miserable end.

@1032
Courtesy name Xianyi. Second son of Yuan Shao. He fought Cao Cao to shield his brother Yuan Shang, who lost the succession struggle to Yuan Tan. His wife Lady Zhen, famed for her beauty, later married Cao Pi and bore Cao Rui.

@1033
Courtesy name Xianfu. Third son of Yuan Shao, favored by his father for his handsome looks. Yuan Shao died before naming him heir, which sparked a succession struggle and caused the fall of the Yuan clan.

@1034
Courtesy name Xiansi. Eldest son of Yuan Shao. After his father's death he opposed his brother Yuan Shang, who inherited by a false will. He beat Yuan Shang and tried to revive the Yuans, but Cao Cao killed him.

@1035
Courtesy name Xuande. Descendant of Liu Sheng, Prince Jing of Zhongshan, of the Former Han. His charm drew many heroes, and he founded Shu and became emperor. He never achieved his dream of restoring the Han and died at Baidi.

@1036
Courtesy name Gongming. One of Cao Cao's Five Great Generals. When relieving Fancheng, he boldly broke through the strongly guarded enemy fortress, ringed ten and twenty deep, amazing Cao Cao with his courage.

@1037
Governor of Beiping. He studied under the same teacher as Liu Bei. Frontier tribes feared him as the "White Horse General." He fought Yuan Shao for Jizhou but was crushed at Yijing and killed himself with his family.

@1038
Courtesy name Yuanzhi. A student of Sima Hui. He served Liu Bei as strategist, but when Cao Cao seized his mother he reluctantly went over to Cao Cao. On parting from Liu Bei, he recommended Zhuge Liang, the Sleeping Dragon.

@1039
Courtesy name Yidu. Younger brother of Kuai Liang and descendant of the Qin-Han strategist Kuai Tong. Cao Cao said, "Gaining Kuai Yue pleases me more than gaining Jingzhou," so great was his wisdom.

@1040
Courtesy name Zirou. Elder brother of Kuai Yue; both aided Liu Biao. He ambushed Sun Jian during the attack on Xiangyang and killed him. He offered many plans, but Liu Biao used few of them.

@1041
Courtesy name Wenhe. A genius strategist compared since youth to great strategists of the past, who used his schemes under Cao Cao. Cao Pi could take the throne only thanks to Jia Xu.

@1042
Courtesy name Fengxiao. Cao Cao's staff officer, whose sound judgment always led to victory but who died young of illness. Cao Cao grieved greatly and, even after the defeat at Chibi, sighed, "If only Guo Jia were alive..."

@1043
Courtesy name Wenruo. Gifted as an aide to a king, he was prized by Cao Cao as "my Zhang Liang." He always offered sound strategy and, at Guandu, encouraged a wavering Cao Cao and led him to victory.

@1044
Courtesy name Kongming. Chancellor of Shu-Han, nicknamed the Sleeping Dragon. He served Liu Bei after the Three Visits and was unbeaten in strategy. After Liu Bei died unfulfilled, he gave all selflessly and launched the Northern Expeditions.

@1045
Courtesy name Ziyu. A Wu advisor and elder brother of Zhuge Liang. His gentle, sincere nature earned Sun Quan's strong trust and he served him well. Often sent to Shu, he worked to ease relations between Shu and Wu.

@1046
Courtesy name Zilian. Cousin of Cao Cao, who joined his uprising with Cao Ren. A mainstay of Cao Cao's army in many theaters, he was a brave general who always risked his life to protect Cao Cao.

@1047
Courtesy name Zijian. Fourth son of Cao Cao. A gifted poet from childhood, loved by his father for his literary talent. Under Cao Pi, however, he was treated coldly and could not use his gifts.

@1048
Courtesy name Zhongda. A great strategist who served four generations of the Cao house. His genius fought Kongming evenly. He later staged a coup, seized real power, and laid the foundation of the Jin dynasty.

@1049
Courtesy name Yongnian. Under Liu Zhang. To stop Zhang Lu's march on Hanzhong, he urged Liu Bei to enter Shu and gave him a map of Shu. That map brought on the age of the Three Kingdoms.
