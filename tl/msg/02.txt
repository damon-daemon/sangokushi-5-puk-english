@2000
Governor of Kuaiji. He later joined Cao Cao and, with Hua Xin, pressed Emperor Xian to abdicate. He challenged Zhuge Liang to a war of words, was crushed, and died of rage. The "Later Memorial" calls him a foolish official.

@2001
Governor of Changsha. Hot-tempered and quick to kill, he was hated by the people. Suspicious that his man Huang Zhong had not shot Guan Yu, he tried to execute him, but Wei Yan cut him down.

@2002
Courtesy name Wenjie. Inspector of Jizhou. When Yuan Shao's scheme took Jizhou from him, he fled in terror, abandoning his family, to Zhang Xiu. A timid man, he lacked the capacity to survive the age of chaos.

@2003
Governor of Dongjun and elder brother of Da Qiao, wife of Sun Ce, and Xiao Qiao, wife of Zhou Yu. He joined the anti-Dong Zhuo coalition at Hulao Pass. Refusing Yan Province Inspector Liu Dai's request for grain, he was killed by Liu Dai.

@2004
Governor of Wuling. When Zhang Fei invaded southern Jingzhou, he sortied against the advice of his man Gong Zhi. Unable to match Zhang Fei, he retreated and was shot dead by the turncoat Gong Zhi.

@2005
Bandit chief who styled himself Virtuous King of Eastern Wu and ruled Wu Commandery. With Wang Lang's help he resisted Sun Ce's invasion but was defeated. While fleeing he was killed by his own man Dong Xi.

@2006
Courtesy name Gongxu. Inspector of Yu Province. He answered the anti-Dong Zhuo call and marched on Luoyang as the third army. Fond of elegant talk, he was said to "breathe as if making flowers bloom on dead wood."

@2007
Courtesy name Wenju. Governor of Beihai, a man of letters among the "Seven Scholars of Jian'an" and a descendant of Confucius. He served Cao Cao with talent worthy of his blood, but his excessive sense of justice got him killed by Cao Cao.

@2008
Courtesy name Yuanzhong. Emperor Ming of Wei, who defended the state against fierce Shu and Wu attacks. In later years he ignored his aides' warnings and indulged in pleasure, weakening the state. He died leaving Sima Yi in charge.

@2009
Courtesy name Mengde. A rare genius in letters and arms who, with superior strategy and foresight, crushed one warlord after another. He rose to Chancellor of Han and laid the foundation of Wei.

@2010
Courtesy name Zihuan. Second son of Cao Cao. After his father's death he became King of Wei, then forced Emperor Xian to abdicate and took the throne, founding the Wei dynasty. He died leaving Sima Yi and Cao Xiu in charge.

@2011
Courtesy name Wentai. Descendant of the strategist Sun Tzu. He joined the anti-Dong Zhuo coalition and was first into Luoyang. On Yuan Shu's orders he attacked Liu Biao's Xiangyang, fell into a trap, and was killed in battle.

@2012
Courtesy name Bofu. Eldest son of Sun Jian. He took up his fallen father's cause and pacified Jiangdong, laying Wu's foundation. Called the "Little Conqueror," he was assassinated young, leaving his dream to his brother Sun Quan.

@2013
Courtesy name Zhongmou. Second son of Sun Jian, who carried on the work of his father and brother. With wise use of talent and firm decisions he won at Chibi. He later founded the Wu dynasty and became its first emperor.

@2014
Governor of Guiyang. He surrendered to Zhao Yun, who invaded southern Jingzhou. As they shared a surname, age and hometown, they hit it off, and he offered Zhao Yun his brother's widow; Zhao Yun refused it as improper.

@2015
Courtesy name Gongqi. Master of the Five Pecks of Rice sect, who founded a religious state in Hanzhong. Beaten by Cao Cao's invasion, he was praised for not burning his treasury and was warmly received after surrendering.

@2016
Inspector of Xu Province, one of the lords in the anti-Dong Zhuo coalition. When Liu Bei came to help against Cao Cao's invasion, he asked Liu Bei to rule Xu Province in place of his aged self, and died.

@2017
He seized the army and government by exploiting He Jin's purge of the eunuchs. He deposed the young emperor and installed Emperor Xian, among countless other crimes. He is also the man who opened the Three Kingdoms era.

@2018
A man of Dong Zhuo. After Dong Zhuo was assassinated he killed Wang Yun with Guo Si, claiming revenge. Holding Emperor Xian, he ruled the court, but fell for Yang Biao's divide-and-rule plot, fought Guo Si, and lost power.

@2019
Courtesy name Junlang. Governor of Youzhou and descendant of Prince Gong of Lu of Han. He welcomed Liu Bei, who came with a volunteer army against the Yellow Turbans, as a nephew and sent him to Qingzhou. It was Liu Bei's first battle.

@2020
Courtesy name Jiyu. Son of Liu Yan. He turned to Liu Bei against Zhang Lu's invasion but brought Liu Bei's invasion instead. His officers urged resistance, but he surrendered, pitying the people who would suffer.

@2021
Courtesy name Gongsi. Son of Liu Bei and the second emperor of Shu. He doted on the eunuch Huang Hao and drowned in drink and women; the state declined and he surrendered to the invading Wei. In China his name means a fool.

@2022
Governor of Lingling in southern Jingzhou. He sent his son Liu Xian and Xing Daorong against Liu Bei's invading army but lost. He surrendered, and Zhuge Liang ordered him to stay on as Governor of Lingling.

@2023
Courtesy name Jingsheng. One of the "Eight Eminent Men of Jiangxia." Suspicious and indecisive, he missed the chance to take the realm. His family feud later gave Cao Cao an opening to invade.

@2024
Courtesy name Zhengli. Inspector of Yang Province. When Sun Ce attacked, he failed to use the able general Taishi Ci and was beaten. The "Later Memorial" cites him as an example of a sloppy official.

@2025
Courtesy name Wenze. Cao Cao trusted his stern loyalty and his feats at Guandu and elsewhere, ranking him among the Five Great Generals. At Fancheng he lost to a flood attack and surrendered to Guan Yu.

@2026
Courtesy name Bodao. He held the key post of Chencang with only 2,000 men and, with skillful command, repelled the genius Zhuge Liang's army of ten times his number, unfazed by his tactics.

@2027
Courtesy name Wenqian. A civil official under Cao Cao, but bold, he always led the charge in battle. He later became a soldier and then one of the Five Great Generals, rich in practical experience.

@2028
Adviser to Yuan Shao, who always clashed with the cautious Ju Shou. He argued for attack at Guandu but misjudged during the battle and was crushed. After Yuan Shao's death he followed Yuan Tan, resisted Cao Cao, and fell in battle.

@2029
Courtesy name Zhongquan. Eldest son of Xiahou Yuan. When Sima Yi had Cao Shuang killed, he feared for his life and fled to Shu. As Jiang Wei's right arm, he fought a desperate battle with the Wei general Deng Ai.

@2030
Courtesy name Anguo. Second son of Guan Yu. He swore brotherhood with Zhang Bao. At the Battle of Yiling he fought hard to avenge his father. Zhuge Liang rated him highly for his valor, worthy of a famous general's son.

@2031
Third son of Guan Yu. He was recovering from wounds when Wu took Jingzhou, then joined Zhuge Liang's southern campaign. Given the vanguard, he fought the barbarian king Meng Huo with his father's valor.

@2032
Courtesy name Wenyue. He raised an army with Ma Chao, son of his sworn brother Ma Teng, to avenge Ma Teng and fought Cao Cao well, but fell for Jia Xu's divide-and-rule plot, turned against Ma Chao, and was forced to surrender to Cao Cao.

@2033
Courtesy name Boyue. Zhuge Liang saw his talent and taught him the secrets of warfare. On his deathbed Zhuge Liang entrusted Shu's future to him, and Jiang Wei fought to the very end to fulfill Zhuge Liang's will.

@2034
Courtesy name Ziyuan. Liu Zhang's brother-in-law. He surrendered when Liu Bei invaded Shu and then served Liu Bei, joining the Northern Expeditions. His sister became Liu Bei's wife (Lady Wu), mother of Liu Li and Liu Yong.

@2035
Son of Gongsun Kang. Taking advantage of the turmoil between Wei and Wu, he styled himself King of Yan and broke away from Wei. He was no match for the army led by Sima Yi and was cut down by his own men as he fled.

@2036
One of the notables summoned when Liu Biao became governor of Jingzhou. When Liu Biao died, he backed Liu Cong as heir and sold Jingzhou to Cao Cao. At the Battle of Chibi he was given command of the navy.

@2037
Courtesy name Ziyuan. Eldest son of Sima Yi, skilled in strategy and as intelligent as his father. After his father's death he held real power in Wei, but his high-handedness, such as deposing Cao Fang, led to the rebellion of Wuqiu Jian and Wen Qin.

@2038
Courtesy name Zishang. Second son of Sima Yi, more thoughtful than his brother and skilled at winning hearts. After his brother's death he took over the army. His conquest of Shu earned him the rank of Chancellor and he became emperor in all but name.

@2039
Courtesy name Youping. Brave and trusted by Sun Quan, he protected his lord at Xuancheng despite twelve wounds all over his body. At a banquet Sun Quan reportedly pointed to each scar and told of his courage.

@2040
Courtesy name Gongjin. Known as Handsome Zhou. On the dying wish of his friend Sun Ce he helped the young Sun Quan, and as Wu's greatest mind became a pillar of the state. At Chibi he repelled Wei's great army with fire.

@2041
Courtesy name Gongyan. Zhuge Liang recognized his calm composure and capacity to run the government and made him Chancellor, the key post. After Zhuge Liang's death he supported Shu well.

@2042
Courtesy name Shiji. Youngest son of Zhong Yao. Clever from childhood, he became Sima Zhao's right arm and was ordered to conquer Shu. After Shu fell, Jiang Wei, who had surrendered, stirred his ambition and he rebelled in Shu.

@2043
Courtesy name Yuanxun. Eldest son of Zhuge Jin. Sun Quan expected much of his talent and Zhuge Ke rose to the occasion in military and civil affairs. After Sun Quan's death he was given full control of the state.

@2044
Courtesy name Gongxiu. Cousin of Zhuge Liang. He rebelled with Wu's help against Sima Zhao's tyranny but failed and met a heroic end. Several hundred of his men died with him, saying, "For Lord Zhuge."

@2045
Chief adviser of Yuan Shao. He offered plans twice at Guandu, but Liu Ye obstructed them. After Yuan Shao's death he backed Yuan Shang, causing the bitter fight with Yuan Tan that helped destroy the great Yuan clan.

@2046
Courtesy name Wenlie. Cao Cao's kinsman, praised by him as "the thousand-li colt of my house" and given an army. In the war against Wu he failed to see through the false surrender of the Wu general Zhou Fang and suffered a crushing defeat.

@2047
Courtesy name Zidan. Cao Cao's kinsman, entrusted with Wei's army by Cao Pi's will. As commander against Zhuge Liang's Northern Expeditions, he fortified the key points and held off the invasion well.

@2048
Courtesy name Zhaobo. Eldest son of Cao Zhen, entrusted by Cao Rui with Cao Fang. He pushed Sima Yi into a post without power and seized control, but Sima Yi caught him off guard and had him executed.

@2049
Adviser to Yuan Shao. His skillful plans led Yuan Shao to victory over Gongsun Zan. At Guandu he advised a war of attrition but was rejected.
